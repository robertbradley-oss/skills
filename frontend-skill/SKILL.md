# Visual direction

Establish a coherent visual direction for new or substantially redesigned web experiences. Begin with the brief, audience, content, and any existing brand system; use a short internal visual thesis when it clarifies choices.

Favor strong composition, purposeful imagery, readable hierarchy, restrained copy, and deliberate spacing. Robert often prefers cardless layouts, a small color palette, and distinctive typography; treat these as preferences, not prohibitions against a suitable established design.

## Marketing and editorial

Give each section a clear job and the first viewport a strong focal point. Use full-bleed imagery when it supports the brief; preserve intentional framing or split layouts when they work. Balance brand, promise, proof, and action according to the content. If the brief calls for a viewport-filling hero, account for persistent header height and mobile browser chrome.

## Product surfaces

Start with the working surface: tasks, controls, data, and status. Use utility labels and concise explanations of behavior or freshness. Do not add marketing heroes to operational tools without a reason in the brief. Cards are appropriate when their grouping or interaction helps users.

## Motion and delivery

Use motion only for useful feedback, continuity, orientation, or deliberate atmosphere; no fixed animation quota. Respect reduced motion and repeated-use speed. Use `emil-design-eng` for detailed interaction work and `frontend-ui-engineering` for implementation where relevant.

Build and inspect the requested result, refine the composition against real content and viewports, and complete appropriate verification. Avoid compulsory planning documents or review pauses when implementation is authorized.
